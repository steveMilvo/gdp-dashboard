# The History of Mildura — A Sourced Research Document

> Compiled as a research reference for a topographically-grounded, real-history
> strategy game set in Mildura and the Sunraysia district, Victoria, Australia.
> The goal is a game that doubles as a quiet learning tool: history taught
> through mechanics, consequences, and discoverable detail rather than lectures.

## How to read this document

- Facts are drawn from authoritative sources (Australian Dictionary of
  Biography, National Museum of Australia, Victorian Heritage Database,
  Engineers Australia, Monument Australia, the Reserve Bank of Australia, and
  Mildura Rural City Council). Source URLs are listed per section.
- ⚠️ marks a **conflict or unverified claim** — treat these as provisional and
  confirm against a primary source before relying on them.
- Dates in **bold** are the load-bearing chronology for the game's timeline /
  build-date gating.

### Open items still to confirm against primary sources

The richest unresolved questions point to **Trove** (digitised *Mildura

- The **materials / foundry** of the original Chaffey fountains.

## 1. Deep Time & the River People (pre-1830)

- The **Latji Latji** (also spelled Latje Latje / Ladji Ladji) are the
  traditional owners of the Mildura area, occupying the southern bank of the
  Murray from Chalka Creek to Mildura and ranging roughly 80 km (50 miles)
  south. Neighbouring nations include the **Barkindji** (across the river),
  **Kureinji, Dadi Dadi,** and **Madi Madi**.
- The Latji Latji name is said to derive from their word for "no" — a common
  naming convention among Murray River nations.
- Aboriginal occupation of the area runs to tens of thousands of years (sources
  cite figures from 40,000 to 60,000+ years). The Murray's wetlands and
  floodplains were a reliable, year-round source of food and water.
- "**Mildura**" derives from a Latji Latji word, most commonly glossed by
  settlers as "**red earth**" / "**red rock**." ⚠️ Alternative recorded meanings
  include "red earth and dust" and "sore eyes (caused by flies)" — the exact
  meaning is contested.

## 2. Explorers & Squatters (1830–1886)

- **1830** — **Charles Sturt** navigates the Murrumbidgee into a larger river he
  names the **Murray** (after British Colonial Secretary Sir George Murray). A
  cairn records him passing the Mildura site on about **22 January 1830**.
- **1847** — The **Jamieson brothers (Hugh and Bushby)** are granted the first
  depasturing licence for a pastoral run named "**Yerre Yerre**." The Old
  Mildura Station Homestead is built c. **1847–1850**.
- **1854** — About **10,000 sheep** graze the run; Hugh Jamieson employs
  Aboriginal workers for shepherding and sheep-washing.
- **1858** — The run is renamed "**Mildura**."
- **1870s–80s** — Drought and the rabbit plague break the station. Owner
  **Alexander McEdward** (acquired the property c. 1874–1878 ⚠️ date conflict)
  sells out; the lease passes through the Tapio/Tapalin Pastoral Company
  (liquidated ~1 year later) and reverts to the **Victorian Government by 1884**.
  The run is effectively defunct before the Chaffeys arrive.

### The land: timber, scrub & bush food

- **River Red Gum (*Eucalyptus camaldulensis*)** lines the Murray in large
  stands — the defining tree of the river corridor and the settlers' primary
  building timber.
- Inland from the river is **mallee** (multi-stemmed dwarf eucalypts) and stands
  of hard **belah / belar (*Casuarina pauper*)** — the dry scrub country the
  district is carved out of.
- **Early huts were red gum timber slab huts** — split-slab walls cut from the
  abundant riverine red gum, the standard pioneer construction of the region.
- Before sheep and cattle were established, **early settlers subsisted on bush
  food — kangaroo and emu** — the staple meat until introduced livestock took
  over. (The Latji Latji had drawn on the same river-and-mallee food sources for
  millennia.)
- **River fishing was a key protein source** — the Murray yielded **Murray cod,
  golden perch (callop), silver perch,** and **yabbies / Murray crayfish**. The
  Latji Latji had fished the river for thousands of years, and early settlers
  relied on it too, alongside hunted game.

### The Murray as a highway (the wool-barge economy)

- **1853** — Murray paddle-steamer trade opens (Randell's *Mary Ann*; Cadell's
  *Lady Augusta* reaches Swan Hill, 17 Sept 1853).
- **Wool was the dominant cargo and the settlement's main source of money**,
  hauled by paddle-steamers towing **barges** both **upriver and downriver** to
  rail/port markets. Bales were shipped after spring shearing at high water; at
  peak ~**150 vessels** worked the river system. The river was the colony's only
  real economic artery before the 1903 railway.
- **1882** — The Murray river trade was estimated to be worth around
  **£5 million**. Echuca (rail-linked to Melbourne, 1864) was the chief inland
  port.

## 3. The Chaffey Gambit — Birth of the Irrigation Colony (1884–1890)

### The protagonists

- **George Chaffey** — born **28 January 1848, Brockville, Ontario, Canada**
  (⚠️ some sources say Kingston); died **1 March 1932, Ontario, California**. A
  Great Lakes shipbuilder and marine engineer; invented a new propeller.
- **William Benjamin Chaffey** — born **21 October 1856, Brockville, Ontario**;
  died **4 June 1926, Mildura**. Known later as "The Boss" / Mildura's "Grand
  Old Man."
- In California the brothers founded the irrigation colonies of **Etiwanda**
  (1881) and **Ontario** (1882), pioneering the non-profit "mutual water
  company" model and the first hydro-electric arc light in Southern California
  (Etiwanda, 30 Nov 1882).

### The recruiter and the deal

- **1884** — Victoria appoints **Alfred Deakin** chairman of the Royal
  Commission on Water Supply.
- **1885** — Deakin tours California, meets the Chaffeys, and publishes
  *Irrigation in Western America*.
- **February 1886** — George Chaffey arrives in Melbourne.
- **October 1886** — Agreement to invest **£300,000 over 20 years**. A hostile
  Legislative Assembly (deriding them as "cute Yankee land grabbers") forces
  competing tenders.
- **31 May 1887** — The **Mildura Indenture** is signed (Queen Victoria, the
  Victorian Government, and George and William Benjamin Chaffey), granting
  **250,000 acres**. The brothers take possession in **August 1887**.
  - Terms: **£5/acre** on two 25,000-acre blocks, then up to 200,000 further
    acres at **£1/acre + £1/acre of improvements**.
- **14 February 1887** — A parallel agreement with South Australia (Premier
  Downer) establishes **Renmark**, another 250,000 acres — Australia's first
  irrigation settlement. (The "500,000 acres" sometimes attributed to George
  Chaffey is the **combined** Mildura + Renmark grants.)

### The town

- Surveyed **August 1887**, on a wide-street American grid modelled on
  **Upland/Ontario, California**.
- The *Mildura Cultivator* newspaper begins **May 1888**.
- By **December 1890**, ~**3,300 people** lived in Mildura (1,100 at Renmark).

## 4. The Engineering Marvel — Water Uphill (1888–1892)

> **This is the heart of the whole story — and the core game mechanic.** Mildura
> exists because the farmland sits ~28 m *above* the river. Water must be lifted,
> then gravity-fed downhill. Elevation is the puzzle.

- The Chaffey "**billabong system**" raised Murray water ~**28 m (≈80 ft)** in
  stages, then let gravity carry it through **300+ miles of channels** to the
  high point of every block.
- **Psyche Bend Pumping Station** ("the Psyche pumps") was the first and lowest
  lift. Designed by **George Chaffey in 1889**.
- Its heart is a **1,000-horsepower triple-expansion steam engine** — Chaffey
  based it on an ocean-liner engine room — built by **Tangyes of Birmingham,
  England**.
- **The Tangye story:** Tangyes initially **refused to build the engine**, then
  agreed only "under duress," insisting on a nameplate reading *"Chaffey's
  improved Pumping Engine made by Tangyes for Mildura Irrigation Colony"* to
  avoid liability if it failed.
- ⚠️ **Capacity figures differ:** Heritage Victoria gives **3 pumps at 35,500
  gallons/minute each**; Engineers Australia states "**670 tonnes/minute**." The
  engine was designed for **4 × 40-inch centrifugal pumps**, but only **three**
  were ever connected.
- **King's Billabong** re-lift station: four centrifugal pumps, ~**32,000 gpm**
  combined. Further stations: **Ninety Foot** and **Nichol's Point**.
- ⚠️ **Commissioning dates vary** across sources: 1890 / 1891 / "operational
  1892."
- The Tangye engine ran until **1959**. It is on the Victorian Heritage Register
  (**H0548**), won the IMechE Engineering Heritage Award (2018), and is billed as
  "the only steam-driven Tangye operational pump of its type in the world." It
  was re-commissioned under steam on **11 October 1995** by the Sunraysia Steam
  Preservation Society.
- ⚠️ **No evidence of "Worthington" pumps** at Mildura was found — only Tangye.
  Treat any Worthington attribution as unconfirmed.

Sources: https://vhd.heritagecouncil.vic.gov.au/places/845 ·
https://www.heritage.vic.gov.au/about-us/news/news-stories/spotlight-on-psyche-bend-pumping-station ·
https://www.imeche.org/news/news-article/australian-psyche-bend-pump-station-receives-engineering-heritage-award ·
https://portal.engineersaustralia.org.au/ (Engineering Heritage nomination)

## 5. A Town Takes Shape — Society & Stone (1889–1894)

### Rio Vista (W.B. Chaffey's home)

- Built for **W.B. Chaffey**, completed c. **1891** (design/commencement often
  dated 1889/1890). "Rio Vista" is Spanish for "River View."
- **Queen Anne** style; designed by Mildura architect **E.C. Sharland**, built
  by Adelaide builder John Williams, with imported Canadian joiner **W. Kells**.
- Features: Blackwood timber staircase, double-height stained/etched glass
  window, Italian marble fireplaces, leadlight, embossed wallpapers.
- Gardens landscaped by **Hughan & Glasson** (Ontario Nursery); featured Murray
  Pine, Norfolk and Canary Island pines, jacarandas, date palms, and a
  double-height timber-and-glass Conservatory (dismantled in the 1950s).
- Address: 199–205 Cureton Avenue. On the Victorian Heritage Register
  (**H0729**). The Chaffey family lived there until **1950**; it opened as the
  Mildura Art Gallery in **1956** (by Governor Sir Dallas Brooks) and is now the
  **Mildura Arts Centre**.

### The temperance colony

- The Chaffeys established Mildura as a **temperance colony**, persuading the
  government to bar the public sale of alcohol while allowing private
  consumption. With pubs prohibited, **coffee palaces and members' clubs** became
  the centres of social life — and the clubs neatly circumvented the ban.
- **Mildura Grand Coffee Palace** (tenders **1889**) — later the Grand Hotel.
  A **Wine Licence** was granted in 1915 and a full liquor licence in 1919,
  effectively ending the dry era for the venue. Its original 1889 cellars house
  **Stefano's Cantina** (opened 1991, by chef Stefano de Pieri — not a Chaffey).
- **The Mildura Club** (1894) — exclusive gentlemen's club on the British model;
  W.B. Chaffey a leading figure. Purpose-built clubhouse (cnr Deakin Ave & Ninth
  St) completed Dec 1919, opened 1920.
- **The Settlers Club** (licence **1895**) — first drinks served on "two boards
  supported by a row of barrels." First premises reportedly a **three-roomed
  cottage in Orange Avenue** (per Mildura Council local history); later
  110–114 Eighth Street. A c.1906 postcard of the club survives at the State
  Library of Victoria.
- **Mildura Working Man's Club** (est. **1894**, 100–110 Deakin Ave) — later home
  to the **world's longest bar**: a T-shaped bar (**1938**), rebuilt **1970** at
  **91 m (299 ft)** with **32 beer taps**, Guinness-recorded, dismantled **1995**
  for poker machines. Women first admitted to the bar on 1 April 1980.

## 6. Crash & Reckoning (1893–1897)

- The **1890s depression** hit Victoria hardest in Australian history — real GDP
  fell ~**17% in 1892–93**, peaking with the **1893 banking crisis** (RBA).
- **1893** — The Murray drops to record lows; the harvest is stranded; the
  planned railway is shelved.
- **10 December 1895** — **Chaffey Brothers Ltd goes into liquidation**, owing
  ~**£22,000** in wages and holding ~438,000 acres of unsold land at Mildura and
  Renmark. Banks foreclose on many settlers.
- **1896 Royal Commission** (held largely at **Langtree Hall**, Mildura) found
  the brothers entered the scheme **undercapitalised** and made serious planning
  errors — no actual breach of contract, but damning on financial management.
  ⚠️ Reported figures (declared capital £474,770; only £44,700 paid in cash; land
  valued at £438,094 then revalued at £136,218) should be verified against a
  primary source.
- **George Chaffey leaves for California (1897)**, later engineering the
  **Imperial Valley**. **W.B. Chaffey stays.**

Sources: https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/rdp/2001/2001-07/1890s-depression.html ·
https://australianfoodtimeline.com.au/chaffey-brothers/ ·

## 7. Recovery & the Fruit Empire (1895–1930s)

- **December 1895** — The **First Mildura Irrigation Trust** is constituted by
  Act of Parliament, run by elected grower-commissioners. The growers, in
  effect, take ownership of their own water. (Water engineer **Stuart Murray**
  investigated settler grievances, prompting the takeover.)
- **Wharf** — opened **1892**, extended **1899** with three berthing levels for
  varying river heights.
- **Railway to Melbourne** (~475 km) — first sod Nov 1901; **officially opened
  13 November 1903** (via Ouyen and Red Cliffs). River trade declined
  thereafter, ending in the mid-1930s; the commercial centre shifted from
  river-facing Madden Avenue to Langtree Avenue.
- **Dried fruits & wine** become the economic engine: Chaffey vines from 1888
  (first vintage 1891); W.B. Chaffey founds the Merbein winery/distillery
  (1913–14) and leads the Australian Dried Fruits Association. By the 1930s
  Mildura produced over half of Australia's dried fruit (Sunraysia ≈95% today).
- **Red Cliffs soldier settlement** — after WWI, returned servicemen took up
  irrigated fruit blocks at **Red Cliffs** (scheme developed c. **1919–1921**),
  one of Australia's largest soldier-settlement schemes; the railway (1903) had
  already passed through the site. ⚠️ Widely documented; exact opening
  ceremony date not verified here.
- **"Big Lizzie"** — the giant oil-engined traction engine built by **Frank
  Bottrill** (c. 1915–17, Melbourne) with his patent "Dreadnought" wheels for
  outback haulage; walking-pace slow, she ended up at Red Cliffs where she
  **cleared the mallee for the soldier-settlement blocks** in the early 1920s.
  Preserved at Red Cliffs today. ⚠️ Outline widely documented; specific dates
  and specs not verified here.
- **Salinity** — the long-term curse: a gravitational drainage outfall was built
  by the State Rivers and Water Supply Commission in **1924**, intensified in the
  1930s.
- **Lock 11 / Mildura Weir** (**1927**) raised and stabilised the river level.
- **W.B. Chaffey** — Shire president 1903; **first Mayor of Mildura, 1920**; CMG
  1924; died 4 June 1926. Population: **1,606 (1901) → 6,617 (1933)**. Mildura
  was proclaimed a **city in 1934**.

## 8. Deakin Avenue & the Civic Streetscape

### The Avenue

- Named after **Alfred Deakin**; laid out at the town's founding (**1887**) as
  the spine of the grid.
- Council figures: ~**12 km** long (⚠️ one source says 8 km), road reserve
  ~**60 m**, central median ("centre plantation") ~**20 m**. The median was
  originally a **tramway reservation**, later planted with a double row of shade
  trees reputedly by W.B. Chaffey himself.
- ⚠️ The "longest/widest avenue in the Southern Hemisphere" claim is **not
  corroborated** — prefer the concrete dimensions.

### Monuments & statues (centre plantation)

- **W.B. Chaffey statue** — life-size **bronze on marble**, sculpted by **Paul
  Montford**, **unveiled 1929** (near Ninth St).
- **Mildura Cenotaph (RS&SILA War Memorial)** — white marble WWI soldier on
  granite, cnr Deakin Ave & Ninth St. ⚠️ Unveiling date conflicts: **1923 (Lord
  Forster)** is the stronger account vs. a 1922/Maj-Gen Cox attribution; sculptor
  unconfirmed.
- **Desailly Rotunda** — band rotunda **erected August 1914**, honouring Cr
  George Desailly (Shire President, died in office 1913); designed by **E.C.
  Sharland**.
- **Mildura Avenue of Honour** — double row of **Sugar Gums (Eucalyptus
  cladocalyx)**; an early Chaffey-era planting block dates to ~**1900**.
- No statue/bust of Alfred Deakin himself was found.

### The Carnegie building & the clock (they are linked)

- **Carnegie Free Library** — opened **1908**, funded by a **£2,000 Andrew
  Carnegie grant (1906)**, at 74 Deakin Avenue. One of only **four Carnegie
  libraries in Australia** (with Northcote VIC, Midland Junction WA, Hobart TAS).
  Now the **Carnegie Centre** (Mildura & District Historical Society).
- The original Carnegie building had a **tower**, removed and replaced by the
  **WWI Memorial Clock Tower** on the same spot.
- **Memorial Clock Tower** — dedicated **1921** by Lord Stradbroke (Governor of
  Victoria). **Four-faced clock imported from England**; builder **E.A. Searle**,
  contract **£1,500**; two brass tablets with ~**100 district names**. ⚠️ One
  source says 1922; 1921 is stronger.
- ⚠️ A walking-tour claim crediting the grant request to "Lord Hopetoun" is
  chronologically doubtful — discard unless confirmed.

### Other civic landmarks

- **Langtree Hall** (**1889**) — Mildura's first public hall; hosted the 1896
  Royal Commission. Now a museum (79 Walnut Ave).
- **Mildura Post Office** — first building **1890**, current building largely
  **1923** (cnr Deakin Ave & Eighth St).
- **Former Mildura Base Hospital** — **Art Deco / "Moderne"**, architects
  **Irwin & Stevenson**, opened **30 August 1934** (day after Mildura became a
  city). VHR **H1934**; closed 2000.
- **Sacred Heart (Peace Memorial) Church** — first Mass **18 December 1921**;
  Lombardic Romanesque; renamed "Peace Memorial" on 1969 extensions.

### The fountains (one original Chaffey fountain, three lives)

- **(A)** The **original Rio Vista fountain** stood in W.B. Chaffey's garden.
  **In May 1897, Edward Chaffey — W.B.'s youngest son — drowned in it**; it was
  then turned off and stood idle.
- **(B)** **In 1936** that disused fountain was relocated to Deakin Avenue's
  centre plantation (at Eighth St) as the **King George V Memorial Fountain** (the
  King died 20 January 1936). ⚠️ Donor framing conflicts (Mrs W.B. Chaffey vs.
  "the citizens").
- **(C)** A **1991 replica** was installed at Rio Vista for the town centenary —
  the one visitors see at the Arts Centre today; the **original** is the one on
  Deakin Avenue.
- **(D)** A **second original Chaffey fountain** survives at the **Grand Hotel**.
  Both Chaffey fountains were reputedly brought upriver on the **paddle steamer
  PS Gem**.
- **(E)** The **1985 "Prince and Princess of Wales" fountain** at the Seventh St
  end of Deakin Ave — switched on by **Prince Charles, 29 October 1985**.
- ⚠️ Could not confirm the fountains' materials/foundry (plausibly Victorian cast
  iron) or the original install date.

## 10. Game-design hooks (summary)

These map the history onto strategy-game structures. See a separate design
document for full detail; this is the historical anchor.

- **Eras / Ages:** River Country → The Squatter Run → The Indenture →
  Water Uphill → The Colony → Crash → The Fruit Republic.
- **Master resource: Water** — must be *pumped* (28 m lift) and *gravity-fed*;
  **salinity** is a slow creeping debuff countered by the 1924 drainage works.
- **Other resources:** Wool, Capital (£), Settlers/Labour, Coal/Steam, Dried
  Fruit & Wine.
- **Keystone tech:** the **Psyche Bend Tangye pump** — nothing grows on the high
  terrace until it is built. Topography (real DEM contours) *is* the difficulty.
- **Landmark / wonder buildings:** Psyche Bend Pump, Rio Vista, Carnegie
  Library, Working Man's Club (longest bar), Mildura & Settlers Clubs, Grand
  Coffee Palace, Langtree Hall (the inquiry building), the Wharf, the Memorial
  Clock Tower, the Cenotaph, the W.B. Chaffey statue.
- **Characters / factions:** George Chaffey (the Engineer, leaves mid-game),
  W.B. Chaffey (the Statesman, stays → Mayor), Alfred Deakin (the Patron), the
  Latji Latji (original inhabitants), the squatters (Jamiesons/McEdward), the
  Banks & Royal Commission (the antagonist economic forces).
- **Scripted crises (historically timed):** Drought (1893), Rabbit plague
  (1880s), Banking Crisis (1893), Chaffey liquidation (1895), Royal Commission
  (1896), creeping salinity, temperance vs. liquor-licence politics.
- **"Stealth learning" devices:** mechanics encode lessons (you fail to farm
  until you pump water uphill); an opt-in codex/almanac that fills in as you
  build; a single true sentence on hover; and a movable fountain *asset* that
  travels across the map over 40 years (Rio Vista garden → idle after the 1897
  tragedy → Deakin Avenue, 1936), so an observant player discovers a human story
  with no text at all.
