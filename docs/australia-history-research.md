# Australian History — Sourced Research for "Australia: A Nation Built"

> Research reference for the curriculum-aligned national history game (see
> [`australia-game-vision.md`](./australia-game-vision.md)). Same discipline as
> the Mildura research: authoritative sources, with **⚠️ flags on every
> contested, uncertain, or commonly-misstated point.**
>
> **Sourcing note:** many authoritative sites (ACARA, NMA, AIATSIS, CSIROpedia,
> Wikipedia) returned HTTP 403 to automated fetching, so claims rest on search
> extracts that quote those canonical pages. Canonical URLs are cited so each can
> be verified in a browser. Treat ⚠️ items as provisional until checked against
> the primary source.

## 1. Eras & key dates (the chronology spine)

### Era 1 — Deep Time / First Peoples
- **~65,000 years ago** — Human occupation at **Madjedbebe** rock shelter (Mirarr
  Country, Arnhem Land), the oldest dated evidence in Australia (Clarkson et al.,
  *Nature*, 2017). ⚠️ **Contested** — some archaeologists dispute the date
  (possible sediment disturbance); "at least 65,000 years (debated)" is the safe
  phrasing. The long-accepted minimum is ~50,000 years.
  https://www.nature.com/articles/nature22968

### Era 2 — Macassan trade
- **From at least 1700 until 1906–07** — **Macassan** (Makasar) trepang fishermen
  from Sulawesi sailed annually to Arnhem Land, trading with **Yolŋu** people —
  pre-European international trade. The new Commonwealth banned it post-1901.
  ⚠️ Start date contested (some argue 1600s).
  https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/trade-with-the-makasar

### Era 3 — European encounter & exploration
- **1606** — **Willem Janszoon** (*Duyfken*) makes first recorded European contact
  with the continent (Cape York). https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/janszoon-maps-northern-australian-coast
- **25 Oct 1616** — **Dirk Hartog** lands on the WA coast, leaving an inscribed
  pewter plate.
- **1642 / 1644** — **Abel Tasman** charts Tasmania ("Van Diemen's Land") and
  names the continent "New Holland."
- **1688 / 1699** — **William Dampier**, first Briton ashore (King Sound, 1688);
  charts ~1,400 km of WA coast (1699).
- **22 Aug 1770** — **James Cook** charts and claims the east coast for Britain
  from Possession Island, naming it New South Wales.
  https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/cook-claims-australia

### Era 4 — Settlement / colonisation
- **26 Jan 1788** — **Arthur Phillip** establishes the convict settlement at
  Sydney Cove (First Fleet, 11 ships); the founding of British settlement, now
  commemorated as Australia Day.
  https://digital-classroom.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/first-fleet-arrives-sydney-cove
- **1803** Hobart founded · **1829** Swan River (WA) · ⚠️ **1836** SA, **1851**
  Victoria separates, **1859** Queensland separates — *standard dates, but not
  confirmed against an authoritative URL in this pass; verify before use.*
- **1788–1868** — Convict transportation; **162,000+** convicts. The last ship
  (*Hougoumont*) arrived **9 Jan 1868**.

### Era 5 — Gold, Eureka, self-government
- **1851** — Gold rushes begin (Hargraves, near Bathurst). Victoria's population
  rose from 76,000 (1851) to 540,000 (1860).
- **3 Dec 1854** — **Eureka Stockade** at Ballarat (~22+ miners killed) — a
  foundational symbol of Australian democracy.

### Era 6 — Federation
- **1 Jan 1901** — **Commonwealth of Australia** founded; six colonies federate
  under the Constitution; **Edmund Barton** first PM.
- **1898–99** — Federation approved by popular referendums (a democratic
  founding). **9 May 1901** — first federal Parliament opens (Melbourne).

### Era 7 — World Wars & between
- **25 Apr 1915** — Gallipoli landing (~16,000 ANZAC troops) → the Anzac legend.
- **1929–1932** — Great Depression; unemployment ~32% (1932).
- **3 Sep 1939–1945** — WWII; **19 Feb 1942** first Japanese air raids on Darwin
  (largest attack on Australian soil).
- **1949–1974** — **Snowy Mountains Scheme** (7 power stations, 16 dams, 145 km
  tunnels); ~100,000 workers from 30+ nations (~65% migrants).

### Era 8 — Rights & recognition (modern)
- **21 May 1962** — **Commonwealth Electoral Act 1962** gives all Aboriginal &
  Torres Strait Islander people the federal vote (enrolment optional). ⚠️ **THIS,
  not 1967, is when Indigenous Australians got the federal vote.**
- **27 May 1967** — Referendum (90.77% Yes — the highest ever) amended s51(xxvi)
  and repealed s127 (counting Aboriginal people in the census; Commonwealth
  lawmaking power). ⚠️ **Common myth:** it did NOT grant the vote or citizenship.

## 2. First Nations deep time & respectful representation (critical)

### Facts for the opening era
- **Oldest continuous living cultures** on Earth, supported by archaeology and
  genetics. https://www.australiangeographic.com.au/news/2011/09/dna-confirms-aboriginal-culture-one-of-earths-oldest/
- **Diversity:** **250+ languages** and ~**800 dialects**, **500+ clan
  groups/nations** at 1788 (AIATSIS estimates — not a census).
- **AIATSIS Map of Indigenous Australia** (David Horton, 1996) ⚠️ is explicitly
  **NOT definitive** — boundaries are not fixed; never render group territories as
  hard borders. https://aiatsis.gov.au/explore/map-indigenous-australia
- **Budj Bim** (Gunditjmara Country, SW Victoria) — one of the world's oldest
  aquaculture systems (~6,600+ years; engineered eel traps); **first place in
  Australia World Heritage-listed solely for Aboriginal cultural values (6 Jul
  2019).** https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1577/
- **Madjedbebe** — world's earliest known ground-edge axe (~44,000–49,000 yrs)
  and among the longest grinding-stone records.
- **Cultural ("cool") burning / fire-stick farming** — long-practised land
  management; living practice today (Firesticks Alliance).
- **Trade networks / songlines** moved ochre, pituri, shell, stone, songs across
  the continent.
- **Torres Strait Islander peoples** are a **distinct** people (Melanesian,
  sea-faring) — always name both groups; never subsume one under the other.

### Frontier truth-telling
- University of Newcastle's **Colonial Frontier Massacres Map**: **414+ massacres
  (1788–1930), 10,000+ Aboriginal people killed**; ~half by colonial officials;
  more occurred 1860–1930 than before. https://c21ch.newcastle.edu.au
- **Stolen Generations** (c.1910–1970); the **Bringing Them Home** report (tabled
  26 May 1997, 54 recommendations). https://humanrights.gov.au/bringing-them-home/index.html
- **Uluru Statement from the Heart** (26 May 2017) — Voice, Treaty, Truth. ⚠️ The
  **2023 Voice referendum did NOT pass** (~60% No) — don't imply it was enacted.

### Representation guidance (bake into design)
- **Name specific peoples** (Mirarr, Gunditjmara, GunaiKurnai), not generic
  labels; reserve "Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples" (plural, spelled
  out — never "ATSI") for the collective. Never use blood-quantum terms.
- **No single homogenised "Aboriginal culture"** — show diversity of Country,
  language, custom.
- **Capitalise "Country"** (lands, waters, skies + connection). Distinguish
  **Acknowledgement of Country** (anyone) from **Welcome to Country** (Traditional
  Owner only).
- **Respect ICIP** (Indigenous Cultural & Intellectual Property): stories,
  songlines, designs, language and sacred knowledge are community-owned — seek
  **free, prior and informed consent**, follow protocols, credit/pay communities.
- **Consult & co-design** with relevant communities (AIATSIS, Common Ground,
  local Traditional Owner corporations) for any region depicted.
- **Strengths + truth:** pair deep-time achievement and living continuity with
  honest, age-appropriate treatment of colonisation, frontier violence and the
  Stolen Generations (content warnings; focus on dispossession, resilience,
  survival).
- ⚠️ Avoid the *Dark Emu* "agriculture vs hunter-gatherer" binary (contested by
  Sutton & Walshe, 2021) — rely on **specific peer-reviewed practices** (Budj Bim
  aquaculture, grinding stones, cultural burning) instead.

## 3. Governance & democracy milestones (the civics-upgrade tree)

A clean progression for "governance upgrades":

- **1823/24** — NSW Act 1823 creates the first (appointed, advisory) Legislative
  Council. **1842** — two-thirds of the Council made **elected** (property
  franchise). ⚠️ Don't conflate 1823 (appointed) with 1842 (elected).
- **1850** — Australian Colonies Government Act → path to self-government;
  **1855–56** colonies adopt constitutions / responsible government.
- **1856** — **Victoria: world-first secret ballot** ("the Australian ballot");
  SA same year; a global democratic export. ⚠️ SA followed closely — word "first"
  carefully. https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/secret-ballot-introduced
- **1857** — manhood suffrage (Victoria, Legislative Assembly), linked to Eureka.
  ⚠️ Eureka's *direct causal* role is debated by historians.
- **1894** — **South Australia: women win the right to vote AND stand for
  parliament — a world first** (NZ 1893 gave the vote but not candidacy).
- **1901** — Federation; federal system; first election 29–30 Mar 1901.
- **1902** — **Commonwealth Franchise Act**: (most) women get the federal vote.
  ⚠️ The same Act **excluded Aboriginal people** from the federal franchise — a
  commonly-omitted qualifier. https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/franchise-act
- **1907** — **Harvester Judgment** establishes the "basic wage."
- **1924** — **compulsory voting** federally (turnout ~60% → ~91%).
- **1962** — Aboriginal & Torres Strait Islander federal **vote** (compulsory from
  **1984**). **1967** — constitutional **recognition** (census + Cwlth power).
  ⚠️ Keep these as **two separate nodes** — the single most-misstated point in
  Australian civics.
- **1975** — the **Dismissal** (Governor-General Kerr dismisses Whitlam) — civics
  lesson on reserve powers and Supply.
- **2017** Uluru Statement · **2023** Voice referendum (⚠️ **defeated**, ~60% No;
  verify exact final figures against the AEC tally).

## 4. Australian inventions tech tree (with myth flags)

**Design rule:** several "Australian inventions" are really **Australian
refinements** of an earlier idea — split those into two nodes (generic invention
vs Australian contribution) to stay accurate.

### Communications & electronics
- **WiFi (wireless LAN technique)** — **CSIRO, John O'Sullivan** & team (Percival,
  Ostry, Daniels, Deane); patent filed **1992**, granted 1996. The signal-
  processing math traces to 1970s radio-astronomy (black-hole hunting). CSIRO
  earned **A$430M+** in royalties. ⚠️ **Myth:** CSIRO did **not** "invent WiFi" or
  the internet — it patented **one key technique** essential to 802.11a/g/n (not
  802.11b). https://www.nma.gov.au/explore/collection/highlights/wi-fi-prototype
- **Black box flight recorder** — **David Warren**, Aeronautical Research Labs,
  Melbourne; concept 1954, prototype ("ARL Flight Memory Unit") ~1958; first to
  combine **voice + flight data**. ⚠️ It's orange, not black. Britain adopted it
  first. https://www.dst.defence.gov.au/innovation/black-box-flight-recorder/david-warren-inventor-black-box-flight-recorder
- **Electric drill** — patented **20 Aug 1889**, Melbourne. ⚠️ Patent names
  **three** people (W.B. Brain, A.J. Arnot, F. Baker) — Arnot gets sole credit
  popularly; and it was an **industrial mining rock-drill**, not the handheld
  tool. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_James_Arnot
- **Atomic absorption spectroscopy** — **Sir Alan Walsh**, CSIRO; conceived Mar
  1952, patent 1953, paper 1955 — a global analytical-chemistry standard.
- **Google Maps (origin)** — **Where 2 Technologies**, Sydney (Lars & Jens
  Rasmussen, Noel Gordon, Stephen Ma), began ~2003 as a desktop app; Google
  acquired **2004**; web Maps launched 2005. ⚠️ Frame as "core tech originated in
  Sydney," not "Australia invented Google Maps." (Gmail is **not** Australian.)

### Household & industrial
- **Hills Hoist** — **Lance Hill**, Adelaide, **1945** (mass-produced). ⚠️ **Not
  the original rotary clothesline** — **Gilbert Toyne** patented working designs
  from **1911**; Hill's was an expired-patent design well-timed for the postwar
  suburban boom. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_Toyne
- **Mechanical refrigeration** — **James Harrison**, Geelong; first **commercial**
  ice-making/vapour-compression machine (experiments ~1851–54; patents 1855–57).
  ⚠️ The principle predates him (Perkins, 1834) — "first practical commercial ice
  machine," not "invented refrigeration."
- **Victa rotary mower** — **Mervyn Victor Richardson**, Sydney, **1952**
  ("Peach-Tin Prototype"). ⚠️ Not the first rotary mower (Hall's "Mowhall" 1948) —
  Victa won on cheap, light, single-operator design.
- **Stump-jump plough** — SA, **Smith brothers**; R.B. Smith patent **1877**.
  ⚠️ Credit split with brother **Clarence**; the "Mullens brothers" attribution is
  a confusion (different invention).
- **Winged keel (Australia II, 1983 America's Cup win)** — credited to **Ben
  Lexcen**. ⚠️ **Genuinely disputed** — Dutch engineer Peter van Oossanen claimed
  authorship (2009; rejected by Bertrand/Longley); present both sides.
- **Dual-flush toilet** — **Bruce Thompson / Caroma**, Adelaide, **1980** (revamped
  1993) — water conservation icon.
- **Wine cask (bag-in-box for wine)** — **Thomas Angove**, Renmark SA, patent
  **1965** (cut-and-peg). ⚠️ The **tap** is a separate 1967 **Malpas/Penfolds**
  invention; the generic bag-in-box was **Scholle (USA, 1955)** — split into nodes.
- **The "ute" (coupé utility)** — **Lewis Bandt**, Ford Australia, Geelong,
  **1934**. ⚠️ The "farmer's wife letter" origin is Ford-endorsed tradition (no
  preserved letter). Henry Ford reputedly called it the "Kangaroo Chaser."

### Medical & health
- **Cochlear implant ("bionic ear")** — **Prof. Graeme Clark**, University of
  Melbourne; first **multi-channel** implant surgery **1 Aug 1978** (patient Rod
  Saunders); first **commercial** Nucleus 22-channel implant **Oct 1982** (patient
  Graham Carrick); commercialised by **Cochlear Ltd** (Nucleus); FDA approval
  1985. ⚠️ Clark = first **clinically successful multi-channel** implant, **not**
  inventor of the cochlear implant (single-channel: William House, USA, 1961;
  earlier Djourno/Eyriès, France, 1957).
  https://www.bionicsinstitute.org/about/professor-graeme-clark-ac/
- **Penicillin (development & mass production)** — **Howard Florey**
  (Adelaide-born), with Chain & Heatley at Oxford; **Nobel 1945** (shared with
  Fleming). ⚠️ Florey **developed/mass-produced** it; **Fleming discovered** it
  (1928). Not an Australian invention. Florey featured on the old $50 note.
  (Oxford biochemist **Norman Heatley** devised the purification/assay — "without
  Heatley, no penicillin" — but was excluded from the 3-person Nobel.)
- **Grey-scale ultrasound imaging** — **George Kossoff** & team, Commonwealth
  Acoustic Laboratories, Sydney; early-mid **1970s** (the scan converter). ⚠️
  Ultrasound itself isn't Australian (Ian Donald, Scotland) — the **grey-scale**
  breakthrough is.
- **Gardasil / HPV vaccine** — **Ian Frazer & Jian Zhou**, Univ. of Queensland;
  VLP tech from 1990 (priority patent 1991); vaccine approved **2006**. ⚠️ Zhou
  died 1999, before approval; Frazer = Australian of the Year 2006.
- **Spray-on skin / ReCell** — **Fiona Wood & Marie Stoner**, Royal Perth
  Hospital; from 1993; used after the **2002 Bali bombings**. ⚠️ Co-invented —
  credit **Stoner** too, not Wood alone.
- **Bionic eye** — **Bionic Vision Australia** consortium; first patient (Dianne
  Ashworth) **Aug 2012**. ⚠️ A consortium R&D program / clinical prototype, not a
  single dated commercial invention.
- **Relenza / zanamivir** — **Mark von Itzstein** (Monash) & Biota, ~1989–90; the
  first structure-based-design antiviral, resting on CSIRO/ANU's 1983
  neuraminidase structure (Colman, Varghese, Laver).
- **Electronic pacemaker (pioneering)** — **Mark Lidwill & Edgar Booth**, Sydney;
  revived an infant by cardiac pacing **1928** (~1926–28). ⚠️ One of the earliest
  documented pacing devices; the modern implantable pacemaker came later (1950s–60s).
- **Aspro** — **George Nicholas**, **1915** (brand registered 1917). ⚠️ Aspirin
  itself was Bayer's (Hoffmann, 1897); the Australian claim is the **brand +
  wartime manufacturing process**, not the molecule.

### Aerodynamics & misc (fact-checked)
- **Box kite / aerodynamics** — **Lawrence Hargrave**, **1893**; tethered-kite
  lift 1894. ⚠️ A major aerodynamics contribution, but **not** powered manned
  flight (Wright brothers, 1903).
- ⚠️ **Myths to avoid:** the **Post-it Note** (3M, USA — *not* Australian); "the
  selfie" (the *word's* earliest record is an Australian 2002 forum post, but
  self-portrait photos long predate it — don't claim Australia "invented the
  selfie").

## 5. Australian Curriculum (ACARA v9) mapping

**Code structure (important):** F–6 is integrated **HASS** → codes `AC9HS<year>K..`
(History is a strand within it). Years 7–10 are standalone **History** →
`AC9HH<year>K..`. *(There is no `AC9HH4` — Year 4 is `AC9HS4...`.)*

### Verified content-description anchors
- **Year 4 — First Contacts: `AC9HS4K04`** — effects of contact on First Nations
  Australians after the First Fleet, "viewed by First Nations Australians as an
  invasion." (`AC9HS4K03` — First Fleet/convict establishment.)
- **Year 5 — colonial Australia: `AC9HS5K01`** (causes of colonies after 1800),
  **`AC9HS5K02`** (impact on First Nations, colonists, convicts, environment),
  **`AC9HS5K03`** (role of a significant individual/group).
- **Year 6 — Federation: `AC9HS6K01`** (individuals/events/ideas leading to
  Federation, the Constitution, democracy). ⚠️ The Year 6 citizenship & migration
  descriptors are confirmed as Year 6 History but their exact K-numbers
  (K02/K03) need confirming on the official page.
- **Year 9 (History 1750–1918): `AC9HH9K02`** (European contact/settlement impact
  on First Nations), **`AC9HH9K03`** (causes of WWI / why Australians enlisted),
  **`AC9HH9K08`** (Industrial Revolution).
- **Year 10 (1918–present):** narrative anchors = WWII, the Cold War, **rights and
  freedoms**, migration. ⚠️ Verify codes (e.g. `AC9HH10K02`, ~`K09` First Nations
  rights) on the official page.

### Concepts to weave through (the "learning" layer)
- The **seven historical concepts**: evidence/sources, continuity & change, cause
  & effect, perspectives, empathy, **significance**, **contestability**.
- The **cross-curriculum priority** "Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander
  Histories and Cultures" is organised around **Country/Place, Culture, People** —
  pair content with these.
- **To remove all ⚠️ code flags:** read the K-codes directly off the per-year
  pages at australiancurriculum.edu.au (they load in a browser; they 403 to
  automated fetch).

## 6. How this feeds the game

- **Eras** (§1) → the Age progression. **Key dates** → when buildings/events
  unlock (date-gating, as in Mildura).
- **Governance milestones** (§3) → the **governance-upgrade tree**; each real
  reform is a node granting civic/stability/diplomacy benefits. Keep 1962 and
  1967 as separate nodes.
- **Inventions** (§4) → the **technology-upgrade tree**; each node gives a
  gameplay effect + a true one-line fact, and (where flagged) honestly notes the
  Australian *refinement* vs the original.
- **Learning points → reward boosts:** tie boosts to engaging with §1–§4 content;
  use the seven historical concepts (§5) as the pedagogical frame (especially
  **contestability** — the ⚠️ items are teaching moments, not problems).
- **First Nations era & framing** (§2) is **foundational, not a prologue** — and
  must be built with consultation/consent (ICIP), accurate naming, and
  truth-telling.
